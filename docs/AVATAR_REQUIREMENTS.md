# Dragon Mastery Avatar Requirements

Art designer guide for the five-tier dragon progression in Learning Lair. Each avatar represents a child's growing mastery of a math operation (1-12).

**Style foundation:** Hand-drawn, sketch-journal aesthetic. All dragons should feel like they're illustrated in a child's field notebook with pencil lines, watercolor washes, and crayon details. No digital perfection or slickness.

**Brand palette reference:**
- `--charcoal` (#3d3528): primary ink lines, deep shadows
- `--kraft` (#a07859): brown outlines, sketch lines
- `--rose` (#d97474): warmth accents, energy
- `--sage` (#7d9d6c): growth, readiness
- `--mustard` (#d4a957): celebration, completion
- `--lavender` (#c79bb8): mastery glow (sparingly)

---

## Tier 1: "Just Starting" (`dragon-new.png`)

**Stage:** Dragon egg or proto-dragon outline — barely formed.

**Visual description:**
- A faint, minimalist outline of a rounded, egg-like form.
- Sketch-quality: pencil lines only, no fills. Barely visible features — maybe a hint of an eye or small spike outline.
- Size: 56×56px container; the outline should be delicate and small within that space (leaving breathing room).
- No color fills yet — just warm kraft-brown or light charcoal pencil strokes on cream paper.
- Personality: tentative, hopeful, just-arrived. Like the first lines a kid draws when starting something new.

**Technical notes:**
- Light opacity/stroke weight to emphasize "not yet here."
- Should read as unfinished without looking broken.
- Minimal detail — aim for 3–5 key lines (head outline, body curve, hint of wings or tail).

---

## Tier 2: "Keep Practicing" (`dragon-learning.png`)

**Stage:** Early sketch, slightly more defined but still clearly in-progress.

**Visual description:**
- The dragon form is now slightly more visible, but still lightweight and sketchy.
- Simple watercolor wash (very pale rose or sage) fills the shape — hint of color but still translucent.
- Small facial features appear: one or both eyes (simple dots or small circles), a tiny open mouth.
- Body outline firmer but still hand-drawn and slightly wobbly — not perfect geometry.
- Size: slightly more prominent in the 56×56px space (maybe 60–70% of container).
- Tail and one or two small spikes are now faintly visible.
- Personality: curious, emerging, still figuring things out. Like the second or third draft when a sketch starts to take shape.

**Technical notes:**
- Watercolor effect: uneven color fill, soft edges, maybe slight paper texture visible.
- Sketch lines (kraft-brown or charcoal) over the wash.
- Still intentionally unpolished — let the hand-drawn quality shine.
- Eyes should be kind and non-threatening (no sharp pupils, keep it gentle).

---

## Tier 3: "Getting There" (`dragon-practicing.png`)

**Stage:** Cute, completed baby dragon / hatchling — fully formed and adorable.

**Visual description:**
- A small, round-bodied dragon with a hatchling / chibi charm — think gentle and approachable.
- Posture: standing upright or slightly playful, wings folded at its sides or half-open.
- Head: proportionally larger than body (young dragon), with big friendly eyes (expressive but not intimidating), small snout, delicate horns or ear-like spikes.
- Body: soft, rounded forms. Belly, chest, and limbs should feel plump and cuddly.
- Color: warm pastels (light rose, pale peachy-pink, soft cream, or muted sage green). Single dominant color or two complementary colors from the brand palette.
- Details: tiny claws, a happy expression (small smile or peaceful expression), maybe a small flame wisp or spark near the mouth.
- Personality: innocent, joyful, learning. The "aww" dragon — kids see it and want to nurture it.

**Technical notes:**
- Fully rendered with consistent shading/coloring (no longer sketchy or washed).
- Use soft edges and subtle gradients (watercolor-style blending, not photorealistic).
- Still maintain hand-drawn linework quality — no perfectly smooth curves.
- 56×56px; dragon fills ~70–80% of the space comfortably.
- Should pair well with the brand grid and tile aesthetic.

## Tier 4: "Almost Mastered" (`dragon-strong.png`)

**Stage:** Teenage dragon, more power and personality — magic awakening.

**Visual description:**
- A more mature dragon form: taller, with visible muscle definition and presence.
- Wings: now fully spread or in motion (suggestion of power and flight).
- Head: longer snout, visible horns or horns with tips, more expressive eyes (determined, wise, confident).
- Posture: dynamic — perhaps mid-leap, breath-ready stance, or wings unfurling.
- Color: richer, more saturated color palette — deeper rose, warm peachy-orange, sage-green accents, or sunset tones. Introduce secondary colors (e.g., rose + mustard, or sage + rose).
- Magic/Energy effects: small sparkles, a faint glow around the dragon, wisps of magical smoke or flame, stars or light particles nearby.
- Details: claws now visible and defined, scales hinted at (subtle line work), movement lines suggesting action or power.
- Personality: growing confidence, magical awakening, approaching true skill. Kid feels proud of their dragon — it's becoming impressive.

**Technical notes:**
- More detail than Tier 3, but still sketch-journal style (not hyper-detailed).
- Watercolor washes + pen linework combination.
- Magical elements (sparkles, glow) should be subtle and tasteful — colored dots, small stars, soft halos.
- 56×56px; dragon is active and fills most of the space, possibly extending slightly beyond calm center.
- Animation-ready: should read well at small size but stand out when magnified.

## Tier 5: "Mastered!" (`dragon-mastered.png`)

**Stage:** Legendary, full-power dragon — mastery achieved.

**Visual description:**
- A large, majestic dragon in full glory: powerful wings spread, commanding presence.
- Head: noble and wise, strong horns, keen eyes (proud, intelligent), noble expression (not scary, but impressive).
- Body: muscular, scaled, fully developed. Posture suggests strength and grace — perhaps rearing up, breathing flame, or in flight.
- Wings: fully extended or wrapping around the dragon, detailed feather-like or membranous texture.
- Tail: long, flowing, possibly curled with intention or power.
- Color: deep, rich jewel tones (dark rose-red, deep burgundy, forest sage, or rich gold accents). Introduce metallics or shimmers (gold, bronze, or copper highlights for treasure/wealth feel).
- Magic/Elemental power: dramatic and celebratory.
  - Flames or golden fire flowing from breath or wings.
  - Bright sparkles, starburst effects, or magical auras in rose, mustard, or lavender.
  - Perhaps gems or treasure elements nearby (subtle nod to the wholesome "dragon hoard" theme).
  - Glow effects around the entire dragon (soft halo or radiance).
- Details: visible scales with shading, individual claws, texture in wings, ornamental details (crowns, jewels, or natural ridges that suggest power).
- Personality: triumphant, wise, protective. The dragon the kid has earned — a companion and symbol of their mastery. Should feel celebratory without being garish.

**Technical notes:**
- Most detailed tier, but still journal-style (hand-drawn, not photorealistic).
- Rich layering: base shapes + watercolor washes + detailed linework + magical effects (sparkles, flame, glow).
- Flame and sparkle effects should be bright and joyful (rose, mustard, gold, lavender, not dark or scary).
- 56×56px; dragon is large and dynamic, fills container fully and commands attention.
- Should feel like a reward — when a kid sees this dragon appear, they feel accomplished.

## Technical Specifications

**Dimensions:** All dragons render in a 56×56px square (48×48px on mobile).

**Background:** Transparent (PNG with alpha channel).

**File format:** PNG, optimized for web (use appropriate compression, aim for <100KB per image for Tier 3–5).

**Rendering context:**
- Dragons sit in a rounded card cell (16px border-radius) with a subtle drop shadow.
- Cell background color changes by tier (cream → pale rose → warm mustard → soft sage → golden mustard with glow).
- Dragon is centered and scales to fit comfortably within the container.

**Brand alignment:**
- No dark/occult themes. All dragons are wholesome and bright.
- Personality progression: innocent → curious → joyful → confident → triumphant.
- Handmade, sketch-journal aesthetic throughout — no digital perfection.
- Palette: warm creams, rose, sage, mustard, gold, lavender sparingly.

## Usage in Interface

**Learning Lair grid:** A 4×3 grid of numbers (1–12) displays one dragon per cell. Children see their mastery level at a glance via dragon appearance.

**Legend:** Below the grid, a small legend shows all five dragon tiers with labels:
1. Just starting
2. Keep practicing
3. Getting there
4. Almost mastered
5. Mastered!

**Background colors by tier** (for reference during illustration):
- Tier 1 (new): `#f7eed4` → `#efe2bf` (light cream gradient)
- Tier 2 (learning): `#f6dede` → `#efc9c9` (pale rose gradient)
- Tier 3 (practicing): `#f6ecd2` → `#eedcae` (warm cream-yellow gradient)
- Tier 4 (strong): `#e6efd9` → `#cfe0bb` (soft sage-green gradient)
- Tier 5 (mastered): `#f3e4ad` → `#e8c873` (golden mustard gradient with glow effect)

## Inspiration & Tone

- **Wholesome:** Dragons are friendly guardians, not monsters. They grow alongside the child.
- **Encouraging:** Each tier should make a child proud and motivated to reach the next level.
- **Hand-drawn:** Aim for the feeling of a watercolor-and-pencil illustration in a cherished notebook, not digital rendering.
- **Playful:** Small personality details (expressions, poses, tiny magical touches) make each tier feel alive and rewarding.
- **Growth narrative:** The visual progression from egg-sketch to legendary dragon tells a clear story of mastery and achievement.
