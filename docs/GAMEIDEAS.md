# Game Ideas

Game types for a child learning the 3× table (3×1 through 3×12). All fit Dragon
Math's bright, nature-forward theme.

## 1. Dragon Egg Hatchery (timed answer)

A clutch of 12 speckled dragon eggs sits in a cozy nest. Each egg shows a problem
(e.g. 3×7). The kid taps the answer from a row of mossy stones; a correct answer
makes the egg wobble, crack, and hatch a baby dragon with a happy chirp. Wrong
answers just dim the stone briefly — no penalty, gentle retry. Hatch all 12 to
fill the nest.

- **Teaches:** quick recall under light, friendly time pressure
- **Why it works:** the reward (a new hatchling) is tied 1:1 to each fact, so kids
  feel progress per problem

## 2. Stepping Stones River Crossing (sequencing / skip-counting)

A friendly otter wants to cross a stream. Lily-pad stones float in the water, each
labeled with a number. The kid taps them in 3× order (3, 6, 9, 12 …) to build a
path. Tapping the right next multiple locks the pad in place with a splash; a wrong
pad ripples and sinks back. Reach the far bank to earn a gem.

- **Teaches:** the *pattern* of threes (skip-counting), which builds intuition
  before pure memorization
- **Why it works:** multiplication-as-repeated-counting is the conceptual bridge
  kids need

## 3. Garden Match Meadow (matching / memory pairs)

A flower-bed grid of face-down petals. Flip two: one shows a problem (e.g. 3×4),
the other shows an answer (12). Match the fact to its product and the flowers bloom
permanently with a burst of butterflies. Mismatches gently flip back. Clear the
meadow to grow the whole garden.

- **Teaches:** linking each problem to its product through repetition and recall
- **Why it works:** the memory mechanic forces multiple exposures to each fact
  naturally
